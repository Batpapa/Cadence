Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 2026

Cadence is a personal flashcard application. This policy explains what happens to your data — and, just as important, what doesn't. The short version: no server stores your data. Some features do talk to a server, but only ever in passing, never to keep a copy.

Data Storage

All your flashcard data (decks, cards, study history) and any recorded session audio are stored locally in your browser's IndexedDB. Nothing is uploaded there by default, and Cadence doesn't require an account.

Microphone & Session Recording

If you use the session-recording feature, Cadence asks for microphone access to capture the audio. Tune recognition runs entirely on-device (via FolkFriend, compiled to WebAssembly) — the audio itself is never sent anywhere for that. The recording is saved to your browser's IndexedDB like any other attachment, and it only leaves your device if you explicitly choose to share that session (see below).

Sharing by Key (Ephemeral Relay)

Cadence lets you share a card pack or a recorded session (optionally including its audio) with someone else via a short one-time key, instead of sending a file. This goes through a small relay server the author operates. It works more like a self-destructing drop-off than a server "storing" anything:

  • Whatever you share is held only in the server's memory (RAM) — it is never written to disk.
  • It is automatically deleted after 10 minutes, whether or not anyone downloaded it.
  • It's retrieved with a random 6-character key (letters and digits, ~2.18 billion combinations) — there's no listing or browsing of what's currently shared.
  • Uploads are capped at 100 MB.
  • The content itself is never logged by the application. (The hosting infrastructure does log ordinary request metadata — timestamp, IP, path — the way any web server does; see "Hosting & Network" below.)
  • If a shared session includes its audio, that audio travels through this same relay, under the same rules, for the same 10 minutes.

If the server restarts for any reason (deploys, crashes), anything still held in memory at that moment is lost — which, again, is the point: nothing here is meant to persist.

TheSession.org Import

Searching for or importing a tune from TheSession.org sends that request straight from your browser to their servers — Cadence doesn't proxy it. This means your search terms (and by extension, something about what you're learning) are visible to TheSession.org the same way they would be if you searched their site directly. See their own privacy policy for how they handle that.

IrishTuneInfo Import

Importing a tune from IrishTuneInfo.com goes through the same relay server mentioned above (it also runs a small scraper for this site, since IrishTuneInfo.com has no public API). Your search/import request passes through it; nothing about it is stored beyond normal request handling — see "Hosting & Network" below.

Trending Data Sync

To show tune popularity trends, Cadence fetches public data directly from GitHub (api.github.com and raw.githubusercontent.com, reading the open adactio/TheSession-data repository). These are read-only requests — no data about you or your library is sent, only the standard request metadata any GitHub request carries (IP, etc.).

Google Drive Integration (Optional)

Cadence offers an optional Google Drive sync feature. If you choose to enable it:

  • Cadence uses the drive.file scope, which only gives it access to the files it creates itself in your Google Drive: a data file (cadence-data.json) and a folder next to it (cadence-data-ext).
  • The data file contains your Cadence data (decks, cards, study history, sessions). The folder holds your larger files: session recordings, attachments, and the periodic backup of a recording still in progress.
  • Cadence does not access any other files in your Drive.
  • Your data is never sent to any third-party server — it travels directly between your browser and Google Drive.
  • The Google sign-in script itself is only loaded once you turn this feature on — not on every visit.
  • You can revoke access at any time from your Google Account settings.

Google API Limited Use Disclosure

Cadence's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Cadence only uses Google Drive data to provide and improve the sync feature within the app. It does not use this data for advertising, profiling, or any purpose unrelated to syncing your flashcards.

Android App

This section applies only to the Cadence app for Android (installed from Google Play or as an APK) — not to the web version. Everything above applies to the app as well, with these differences:

  • Where your data lives. Instead of a browser's storage, your data is kept in the app's private storage on your device, which other apps cannot read. Uninstalling the app, or clearing its data in Android's settings, deletes it — use Google Drive sync or an export if you want to keep a copy. If Android's own device backup is turned on, it may include the app's local data, as it does for any app; that backup is handled by Google under your Google account, and the author has no access to it.
  • Permissions.
    • Microphone (RECORD_AUDIO, plus MODIFY_AUDIO_SETTINGS, which the app's built-in web view needs to open it) — used only during a session recording you start yourself.
    • Foreground service of type microphone and wake lock — let a recording carry on with the screen off or another app in front. While it runs, Android shows a persistent notification; the app never listens in the background without it.
    • Notifications (Android 13 and later) — for that recording notification only. Cadence sends no other notifications.
    • Internet — for the features described above.
  • Lock screen. During a recording, the notification and the lock-screen media controls show the elapsed time and the name of the tune currently recognised. Like any media player's, they are visible to anyone who can see your screen.
  • Google sign-in. The app signs in through Google Play services on your device instead of the web sign-in script: you pick your account in Google's own screen, and Cadence never sees your password. Same files, same drive.file scope, same rules as in "Google Drive Integration" above, and access is revoked the same way.
  • Files. Exports go through Android's own "save as" screen, to the place you choose. The app has no permission to read your files or photos: it only opens a file you pick yourself in the system's file picker.
  • Updates. When it starts and when you come back to it, the app checks GitHub Pages (the site that serves the web version, batpapa.github.io) for a newer version of its own code, and downloads it if there is one. These requests carry nothing about you or your data, only the usual request metadata (IP address, etc.). No third-party update service is involved.

Hosting & Network

Cadence is a static site served from GitHub Pages, and the relay/scraper above runs on Render. Like essentially any website, both of these can see ordinary network-level request metadata (your IP address, timestamp, and the path requested) simply by virtue of serving the request — this isn't specific to Cadence and isn't something any website can avoid. Neither logs the actual content of what you share.

Data Retention & Deletion

The author holds no copy of your data, so there is nothing on the author's side to keep or to delete. Your data stays where you put it, for as long as you keep it there:

  • On your device — until you remove it: deleting a user in Cadence's settings removes that user's data from the device; clearing the site's data in your browser, or uninstalling the Android app (or clearing its data in Android's settings), removes everything.
  • In your Google Drive — until you delete cadence-data.json and the cadence-data-ext folder yourself, from Google Drive. Disconnecting Drive in Cadence, or revoking its access, stops any further sync but leaves those files in your Drive.
  • Shared by key — deleted automatically after 10 minutes (see above).

What Cadence Does Not Collect

Cadence has no analytics, no telemetry, no advertising, and no user accounts. It doesn't track you across sessions or devices, and no server — including the relay described above — keeps a durable copy of your data.

Third-Party Services, at a Glance

  • Google Drive — optional, opt-in, only when you enable sync.
  • Google Identity (sign-in) — only loaded once you enable Drive sync.
  • thesession.org — when you search or import from TheSession.
  • The author's relay/scraper (Render) — when you share by key, or import from IrishTuneInfo.
  • GitHub (api.github.com, raw.githubusercontent.com) — read-only, for trending data.
  • GitHub Pages (batpapa.github.io) — Android app only, to check for and download its own updates.
  • Google Play services — Android app only, for Google sign-in when you enable Drive sync.

When Google Drive sync is enabled, your data is subject to Google's Privacy Policy.
